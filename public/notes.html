I Will Turn Their Mourning into Joy

Jeremiah 31–33; 36–39 · Lamentations 1; 3

The plan

One idea runs through everything: the Lord turns mourning into joy. Each story starts with something broken (a burned scroll, a muddy pit, a city in ruins) and ends with what God made of it.

  • 23 slides, about 23 minutes, which leaves a couple of minutes of buffer inside 25.
  • Keep it moving. Each slide has one main question. Take two or three answers, then go on. Don't wait for everyone.
  • Running long? Use the cuts tagged If behind: skip the warm-up on slide 2, skip quiz questions two and three (press → twice to pass one), and read the Ebed-melech list without asking for ideas.
  • Before class: open the deck on the laptop, press F for full screen, and press Home to start at slide 1. Keep this page open on your phone.

Opening

Slides 1–3 · 3 minutes

1

Title

30 sec
  1. "Today starts on the worst day in Jerusalem's history, and ends with a promise: I will turn their mourning into joy. Watch the background: it starts at night. By the end, it's morning."
2

A worst day, turned around

1 min If behind: skip
  1. Both questions appear (two clicks).
  2. "Anyone have a bad day that turned out okay? What turned it around?" Take one or two.
    • Lost a game, won the next one
    • Lost something, then found it
    • New school, then a new friend
    • "My mom," "a friend," "I prayed"
  3. The gold line appears. Read it.
3

An empire is coming

1½ min
  1. "About 600 B.C. Babylon is the superpower of the world. Jerusalem is a small kingdom in its way. A prophet named Jeremiah spends forty years warning them: turn back to God, or the city will fall. Almost nobody listens."
  2. The army's route draws across the map.
  3. "600 B.C., Jerusalem. What other family in the scriptures lived there right then?"
    • Lehi's family
  4. The Lehi line appears.
  5. "Lehi's family got out. Jeremiah stayed."

Story One · The scroll

Slides 4–5 · 3 minutes · Jeremiah 36

4

Chapter: The scroll

15 sec
  1. No need to say anything. Let the title land and press →.
5

The scroll scene

2½ min
  1. "Jeremiah speaks the Lord's words, and his scribe Baruch writes every one on a scroll."
  2. The fire and the king appear.
  3. "It's read to King Jehoiakim, who's sitting by a fire in his winter house."
  4. Pieces fly into the fire.
  5. "Every few columns, he cuts off a piece with a knife and throws it in the fire."
  6. The whole scroll burns.
  7. "Until the whole thing is gone."
  8. "What does the Lord do now?"
  9. "So what does the Lord do?"
    • Punish the king
    • Give up on him
    • "Write it again!" (make a big deal if someone gets this)
  10. The new scroll glows.
  11. "Take thee again another roll, and write in it all the former words… and there were added besides unto them many like words."
  12. "He didn't just replace it. The new one had more. You can't burn God's word away."

Story Two · The pit

Slides 6–8 · 4 minutes · Jeremiah 38–39

6

Chapter: Down in the pit

15 sec
  1. "Now Babylon's army is camped outside the walls, and the city's leaders are furious with Jeremiah."
7

The pit scene

2½ min

The most hands-on slide. Keep the energy up.

  1. "So they lower him on ropes into a deep, dry well. No water. Just mud. He sinks."
  2. The three men appear.
  3. "Then Ebed-melech, an Ethiopian servant in the palace, an outsider, does what nobody else will. He goes to the king and speaks up. The king says: take thirty men and get him out."
  4. The question appears.
  5. "Besides ropes, he brought something else. Guess." Click the answer they call out.
  6. Old rags, with the verse.
  7. "So the ropes wouldn't cut his arms. He didn't just rescue Jeremiah; he thought about how the rescue would feel."
  8. "Everybody grab the rope!" Click Pull (or press →) five times. Each time, the class pulls and says "heave!" The first pull also brings up "Why the rags?"
  9. On the fifth pull he's out, and gold dust falls.
8

Be an Ebed-melech

1½ min
  1. "When Jerusalem fell, the Lord kept Ebed-melech safe 'because thou hast put thy trust in me.'"
  2. The question appears.
  3. "Who might be 'in a pit' around you? Don't say names. Just think."
  4. Five seconds of quiet.
  5. "Your old rags," then the list (four clicks).
  6. "Small things that make help feel kind instead of awkward." Read the list as it appears. If behind: just read it

Story Three · The worst day

Slides 9–11 · 3 minutes · Jeremiah 39; Lamentations 1; 3

9

Chapter: The worst day

15 sec
  1. Slow your voice down. The tone changes here.
10

Jerusalem falls

1½ min
  1. The city cracks and burns.
  2. "The army breaks through. They burn the houses, the palace, and the temple."
  3. People walk away to Babylon.
  4. "Most of the people are marched hundreds of miles away."
  5. The gold line appears.
  6. "Just as Jeremiah warned. But the Lord kept His promise to protect him."
  7. Lamentations 1 appears.
  8. "Afterward, someone wrote a book of sad poems about it, called Lamentations. Listen:" Read the verse slowly.
  9. Let it sit for a moment. Don't rush to the sunrise.
11

But then, morning

1 min

The turning point of the lesson.

  1. "Look at the background." Point to the sunrise. "Same book, two chapters later:"
  2. Read each line as it appears (two clicks).
  3. "…his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness."
  4. The gold line appears.
  5. "Everything's still in ruins, and he's still finding mercy. Every morning is a fresh start from the Lord."
  6. The question appears.
  7. "What's one good thing you noticed today?" Take two quick answers.

The great promise

Slides 14–16 · 3 minutes · Jeremiah 31; 33

14

Chapter: Written in the heart

15 sec
  1. "In the middle of all this, the Lord makes His biggest promise."
15

Stone and heart

1½ min
  1. "Under Moses, the law was carved on stone. Rules on the outside."
  2. The verse appears. Read it.
  3. The words fly from the stone into the heart.
  4. The main idea appears.
  5. "What's the difference between doing what's right because you have to and because you want to?"
    • "Want to" still works when nobody's watching
    • It feels like joy instead of a chore
    • It becomes who you are
16

Call unto me · The Branch

1½ min
  1. Verse 3 appears.
  2. "Call unto me, and I will answer thee, and shew thee great and mighty things, which thou knowest not."
  3. "He said this to Jeremiah while he was still in prison."
  4. A branch grows from the stump.
  5. "After this, Israel had no more kings. David's family tree looked chopped down. But a Branch would grow out of it."
  6. "Who's the Branch?"
    • Jesus Christ
  7. The gold line appears. "He's how mourning turns into joy."
