The only one in the room

  1. Think of a time you were the only one. The only one not laughing at the joke. The only one who left the party. The only one who didn't send the text.

This week: four teenagers, far from home in the biggest city in the world, who would not give in.

Let two or three people share. Keep it light and quick. If nobody volunteers, share one of your own.

Being the only one feels lonely in the moment. Daniel and his friends felt that as teenagers, and Daniel still felt it at eighty.

About 605 B.C. · Daniel 1:1–7

Carried off to Babylon

Babylon was the superpower of the world, like the biggest and richest country today. Its king, Nebuchadnezzar, marched to Jerusalem and took the smartest young people back with him to train at his palace for three years.

Among them: Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. Probably about your age. No parents, no church, and nobody from home watching what they did.

Story One

The King's Table

Daniel 1

Their first test is not dramatic. It is dinner.

The king's meat and wine
Pulse and water
Day 1of ten
Daniel 1:8

But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank.In plain words: Daniel decided ahead of time that he would not eat the king's food or drink his wine.Daniel 1:8

Picture the best food you've ever seen, served by the most powerful person alive, and everyone around you eating it. Daniel had already decided: not for me.

The test · Daniel 1:12–14

Prove thy servants, I beseech thee, ten days; and let them give us pulse to eat, and water to drink.In plain words: Test us. Give us ten days on vegetables and water, then compare.Daniel 1:12

"Pulse" means plain food that grows from seeds: vegetables, grains, beans.

The result · Daniel 1:15–20

At the end of ten days their countenances appeared fairer and fatter in flesh than all the children which did eat the portion of the king's meat.In plain words: After ten days they looked healthier than everyone else.Daniel 1:15

And God made them wise. When the king tested them, they were ten times better than all his experts.

Consider

Daniel decided before the food was ever in front of him.

Like deciding you won't drink before you ever get to the party. When someone hands you a cup, the choice is already made.

What is one thing you have already decided?

Click 1: the ten-day test begins and the day counter runs. Click 2: the pulse plate glows, the king's plate fades. Click 3: the main idea.

The point is not vegetables. It is "purposed in his heart": he decided in advance, when he was calm, so the moment did not get a vote. Deciding in the moment is hard: everyone's watching, you're tired, it smells good.

Likely answers: not drinking, keeping the Sabbath, serving a mission, no pornography, being honest. Affirm every one.

Story Two

The Statue
and the Stone

Daniel 2

Now the king has a dream nobody can explain.

Daniel 2:1–13

The king has a nightmare. He tells his wise men: tell me what I dreamed and what it means, or you all die.

Nobody can do it. The order goes out to kill every wise man in Babylon, and that includes Daniel and his friends.

Daniel 2:17–23

Daniel asks for one night. He and his three friends pray.

He revealeth the deep and secret things: he knoweth what is in the darkness, and the light dwelleth with him.In plain words: God knows the things nobody else can see.Daniel 2:22

The dream · Daniel 2:31–43

A giant statue, made of four metals.

Each part is a kingdom. Babylon is the gold head. Then come the empires that would take over, one after another, like a line of champions each beaten by the next.

Click to build the statue

Daniel 2:34–35

A stone was cut out without hands, which smote the image… Then was the iron, the clay, the brass, the silver, and the gold, broken to pieces together… and the stone that smote the image became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth.In plain words: A stone no person made hit the statue. It crumbled to dust, and the stone grew until it filled the whole earth.Daniel 2:34–35

The meaning · Daniel 2:44

The God of heaven shall set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed… and it shall stand for ever.In plain words: God will set up His own kingdom, and it will never end.Daniel 2:44

Every earthly kingdom ends. God's kingdom does not.

Click 1: the prayer. Click 2: the statue and its gold head. Clicks 3–6: silver, brass, iron, iron and clay. Click 7: the stone rolls from the mountain and the statue shatters. Click 8: the stone grows to fill the screen, with the meaning.

The labels are the usual Latter-day Saint reading: Babylon, then the Medes and Persians, then Greece, then Rome, then the broken-up kingdoms after Rome. No need to belabor it.

"Without hands" means no human built it. The stone is God's doing.

The stone today

The stone is still rolling

The keys of the kingdom of God are committed unto man on the earth, and from thence shall the gospel roll forth unto the ends of the earth, as the stone which is cut out of the mountain without hands shall roll forth, until it has filled the whole earth.Doctrine and Covenants 65:2

The stone is God's kingdom today: the Church of Jesus Christ.

It started in 1830 with six members in a farmhouse. Now it's in almost every country on earth.

Where have you seen it rolling?

A friend who got baptized New temples every conference Missionaries in your own family Your own testimony growing

You are not watching the stone roll. You are part of it.

Click 1: what the stone is. Click 2: the question. Take two or three answers before Click 3 shows examples. Click 4: the gold line.

Joseph Smith taught that this same stone is the Church the Lord restored through him, and Doctrine and Covenants 65 uses Daniel's exact words. "Without hands" means no person built it.

Story Three

The Furnace

Daniel 3

The king heard that God's kingdom would outlast his. His answer: build a golden statue of his own, and make everyone bow to it.

Ninety feet of gold
Cornet, flute, harp…
Daniel 3:1–6

The king builds a gold statue as tall as a nine-story building and calls in every leader in the empire.

The rule is simple. When the music plays, everyone bows. Anyone still standing goes into a furnace.

Daniel 3:7–12

The music plays. Thousands bow.

Three stand.

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego. Everyone can see them. The king is told.

Their answer · Daniel 3:16–18

Our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king. But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up.In plain words: God can save us, and we believe He will. But even if He doesn't, we still won't bow.Daniel 3:17–18

Consider

"But if not."

Their faith wasn't a deal with God: get us out of this and we'll be good. It was: we'll be good either way. Like praying hard to make the team, and still trusting God if you get cut.

What would change if your faith had a "but if not" in it?

Click 1: the music plays and the crowd bows; three gold figures stay standing. Click 2: their answer. Click 3: "But if not."

Spend time on "but if not." It is the heart of the chapter. Faith that depends on getting what you want is a bargain, not faith.

Likely answers: less fear; you can stop bargaining; you can keep going when prayers aren't answered the way you hoped.

Heated seven times hotter
Daniel 3:19

Daniel 3:19–23

The king is furious. He orders the furnace heated seven times hotter.

It is so hot that the soldiers who throw them in die from the heat. The three are tied up and dropped into the flames.

Daniel 3:24–25

Then the king jumps to his feet.

Did not we cast three men bound into the midst of the fire?… Lo, I see four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire, and they have no hurt; and the form of the fourth is like the Son of God.In plain words: We threw in three. I see four, walking around, not hurt, and the fourth looks like the Son of God.Daniel 3:24–25

Daniel 3:26–27

Upon whose bodies the fire had no power, nor was an hair of their head singed, neither were their coats changed, nor the smell of fire had passed on them.In plain words: Not a hair burned. Their clothes weren't scorched. They didn't even smell like smoke.Daniel 3:27

He did not keep them out of the fire.
He was in it with them.

Consider

When has the Lord been with you in a hard thing, rather than taking it away?

The hard thing didn't disappear. The divorce still happened. The friend still moved. The worry is still there. But you weren't alone in it.

The king's confession · Daniel 3:28–29

Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego, who hath sent his angel, and delivered his servants that trusted in him… because there is no other God that can deliver after this sort.In plain words: Their God sent an angel and saved them because they trusted Him. No other god can do that.Daniel 3:28–29

Click 1: the flames roar up, the three men drop in, and a moment later a fourth, glowing figure appears beside them. Click 2: the flames die down; not even the smell of fire. Click 3: the question. Click 4: the king's confession, which is the title of the lesson.

Let the fourth figure sit on screen for a moment before you say anything.

On the question, be ready to share your own example: a loss, an illness, a move, something that didn't go away but that you weren't alone in.

Two proud kings

Nebuchadnezzar

Daniel 4

Is not this great Babylon, that I have built…?Daniel 4:30

Pride is thinking you did it all yourself. He dreams of a huge tree chopped down to a stump. Then it happens to him: he loses his mind and lives outside like an animal for seven years, until he admits God is in charge.

Those that walk in pride he is able to abase.Daniel 4:37

Belshazzar

Daniel 5

Years later, the new king throws a party for a thousand guests and uses the sacred cups stolen from God's temple as party cups. Then a hand appears and writes on the wall.

MENE MENE TEKEL UPHARSIN

Thou art weighed in the balances, and art found wanting.In plain words: You've been tested, and you failed.Daniel 5:27

That same night, Babylon fell to the Persians.

"The most High ruleth in the kingdom of men." (Daniel 4:17) God is the one really in charge.

Quick slide, about 90 seconds. Click 1: the tree becomes a stump and Nebuchadnezzar is humbled. Click 2: Belshazzar's feast. Click 3: the hand writes on the wall, one word at a time. Click 4: the city falls and the verse.

Nebuchadnezzar was humbled and came back. Belshazzar was warned and ignored it. Same God, same lesson, two endings.

If you're behind, skip this slide entirely.

Story Four

The Lions' Den

Daniel 6

A new empire, a new king, and Daniel is now an old man.

The den of lions

Daniel 6:1–9

Daniel is about eighty now, and the king's right-hand man.

The other leaders are jealous. They dig for dirt on him and find nothing. So they set a trap: a new law that for thirty days, nobody may pray to anyone but the king. Break it, and you're thrown to the lions.

Daniel 6:10

He went into his house; and his windows being open… he kneeled upon his knees three times a day, and prayed, and gave thanks before his God, as he did aforetime.In plain words: Daniel went home and prayed three times a day, the same as always.Daniel 6:10

Windows open. Nothing hidden. Nothing changed.

Daniel 6:16–18

Into the den.

The king realizes he's been tricked, but a law is a law. As they seal the den he says, "Your God will save you." Then he goes home and can't eat or sleep.

Morning · Daniel 6:19–23

My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me.In plain words: God sent an angel to shut the lions' mouths.Daniel 6:22

"No manner of hurt was found upon him, because he believed in his God" (6:23).

Consider

Daniel's habit at fifteen was still his habit at eighty.

Like brushing your teeth: you don't decide each night, it's just what you do. Daniel's prayer was like that, and that habit is what saved him.

What habit are you building now that you want to still have at eighty?

Click 1: Daniel 6:10. Click 2: the stone rolls over the den, Daniel is inside, and lions' eyes glow in the dark. Click 3: morning. The stone rolls away, light pours in, and the lions lie down. Click 4: the question.

"As he did aforetime" is the key phrase. The law didn't make Daniel brave; his habits did.

Likely answers: daily prayer, scripture study, church every week, honesty, keeping the Sabbath.

Daniel 7:13–14

I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven… And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom… his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away.In plain words: Daniel saw the Savior coming in the clouds, and He was given a kingdom that lasts forever.Daniel 7:13–14

"Son of man" is the name Jesus used for Himself more than any other. He said the Son of man came to serve, "and to give his life a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45).

The "Ancient of days" is Adam. Before Jesus comes again, Adam will gather his family at a place in Missouri called Adam-ondi-Ahman, and the Savior will come to them (D&C 116; 27:11).

Every kingdom in Daniel falls. Christ's kingdom never will.

Babylon, Persia, Greece, Rome: you need a history book to find them now. The Church is still growing.

Daniel 7 is a vision of the end: beasts rise and fall, then the Son of man comes in the clouds and receives a kingdom that never ends.

Click 1: Jesus used this title for Himself more than any other. Click 2: the Ancient of days is Adam, and Adam-ondi-Ahman is in Missouri. Click 3: the gold line.

Keep this to 90 seconds. The point is simple: the statue falls, the beasts fall, Babylon falls, Persia falls. Christ's kingdom is the only one that lasts.

"Here's the whole lesson in two minutes. Around 605 B.C., Babylon carried off a group of bright teenagers from Jerusalem, including Daniel and his three friends. They were given new names after Babylon's gods and the best food in the empire. Daniel had already decided, before the food was in front of him, that he would not take it. He asked for ten days on plain food and water, and at the end he and his friends were healthier and ten times wiser than anyone in the king's court. Decide before the moment comes.

"The king dreamed of a giant statue made of gold, silver, brass, and iron, every part a kingdom. A stone cut out of a mountain without hands smashed it and grew to fill the whole earth. That stone is God's kingdom, and the Doctrine and Covenants says it's rolling forth in our day. You're part of it.

"Then the king built a golden statue and ordered everyone to bow. Three friends stood. They told him God was able to deliver them, 'but if not,' they still wouldn't bow. They were thrown into a furnace heated seven times hotter, and the king saw four men walking in the fire. They came out without even the smell of smoke. God didn't keep them out of the fire; He was in it with them. The king said, 'There is no other God that can deliver after this sort.'

"Years later, Daniel, now about eighty, kept praying three times a day when a law said he couldn't. He was thrown to the lions, and God shut their mouths, because he believed in his God. The habit he built at fifteen was still there at eighty.

"And Daniel saw the end: every kingdom falls, and the Son of Man, Jesus Christ, comes in the clouds to receive a kingdom that never ends.

"So: decide now. Trust Him either way. Pray like you always have. He is the only God who can deliver, and He will be with you in the fire."
