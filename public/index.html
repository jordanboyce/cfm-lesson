To begin

The only one in the room

  1. Think of a time you were the only one: the only one who didn't laugh, didn't go along, didn't join in.
  2. What made it hard? What made it possible?

This week: four teenagers, alone in the most powerful city on earth, who would not bend.

Let two or three people share. Keep it light and quick. If nobody volunteers, share one of your own.

Point out that being the only one usually feels lonely in the moment. Daniel and his friends felt that at fifteen, and Daniel still felt it at eighty.

About 605 B.C. · Daniel 1:1–7

Carried off to Babylon

Jeremiah warned Jerusalem that Babylon was coming. The first wave came in 605 B.C. King Nebuchadnezzar took treasure from the temple, and a group of the brightest young men to be trained at his court for three years.

Among them: Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. Probably teenagers. Far from home, with no parents, no temple, and no one watching.

Story One

The King's Table

Daniel 1

Their first test is not dramatic. It is dinner.

The king's meat and wine
Pulse and water
Day 1of ten
Daniel 1:8

But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank.Daniel 1:8

The best food in the empire, from the king himself. Daniel decided ahead of time: not for me.

The test · Daniel 1:12–14

Prove thy servants, I beseech thee, ten days; and let them give us pulse to eat, and water to drink.Daniel 1:12

Pulse: plain food that grows from seed. Vegetables, grain, beans. For ten days.

The result · Daniel 1:15–20

At the end of ten days their countenances appeared fairer and fatter in flesh than all the children which did eat the portion of the king's meat.Daniel 1:15

And God gave them "knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom." The king found them ten times better than all his wise men.

Consider

Daniel decided before the food was ever in front of him.

Decisions made in the moment are hard. Decisions made in advance are already made.

What is one thing you have already decided?

Click 1: the ten-day test begins and the day counter runs. Click 2: the pulse plate glows, the king's plate fades. Click 3: the main idea.

The point is not vegetables. It is "purposed in his heart": he decided in advance, when he was calm, so the moment did not get a vote.

Likely answers: not drinking, keeping the Sabbath, serving a mission, no pornography, being honest. Affirm every one.

Story Two

The Statue
and the Stone

Daniel 2

Now the king has a dream nobody can explain.

Daniel 2:1–13

King Nebuchadnezzar has a dream that terrifies him. He demands that his wise men tell him the dream and its meaning, or die.

None of them can. The order goes out to execute every wise man in Babylon, including Daniel and his friends.

Daniel 2:17–23

Daniel asks for one night. He and his three friends pray.

He revealeth the deep and secret things: he knoweth what is in the darkness, and the light dwelleth with him.Daniel 2:22

The dream · Daniel 2:31–43

A giant statue, made of four metals.

Each part is a kingdom. Babylon first, then the empires that would follow it, each one weaker than the last, until the kingdoms crumble into pieces.

Click to build the statue

Daniel 2:34–35

A stone was cut out without hands, which smote the image… Then was the iron, the clay, the brass, the silver, and the gold, broken to pieces together… and the stone that smote the image became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth.Daniel 2:34–35

The meaning · Daniel 2:44

The God of heaven shall set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed… and it shall stand for ever.Daniel 2:44

Every earthly kingdom ends. God's kingdom does not.

Clicks 1–2: the crisis and the prayer. Clicks 3–7: the statue builds from the head down, one metal per click. Click 8: the stone rolls from the mountain and the statue shatters. Click 9: the stone grows to fill the screen, with the meaning.

The labels are the usual Latter-day Saint reading: Babylon, then the Medes and Persians, then Greece, then Rome, then the divided kingdoms of Europe. No need to belabor it.

"Without hands" means no human built it. The stone is God's doing.

The stone today

The stone is still rolling

The keys of the kingdom of God are committed unto man on the earth, and from thence shall the gospel roll forth unto the ends of the earth, as the stone which is cut out of the mountain without hands shall roll forth, until it has filled the whole earth.Doctrine and Covenants 65:2

The stone is the kingdom of God, restored in our day: the Church of Jesus Christ.

Where have you seen it rolling?

A friend who got baptized New temples every conference Missionaries in your own family Your own testimony growing

You are not watching the stone roll. You are part of it.

Click 1: what the stone is. Click 2: the question. Take two or three answers before Click 3 shows examples. Click 4: the gold line.

Joseph Smith taught that this same stone, cut out of the mountain without hands, is the kingdom the Lord restored through him, and Doctrine and Covenants 65 uses Daniel's exact words.

Story Three

The Furnace

Daniel 3

The king heard that God's kingdom would outlast his. His answer: build a golden statue of his own, and make everyone bow to it.

Ninety feet of gold
Cornet, flute, harp…
Daniel 3:1–6

The king builds a golden image ninety feet tall and summons every official in the empire.

The rule: when the music plays, everyone bows. Anyone who does not bow goes into the burning fiery furnace.

Daniel 3:7–12

The music plays. Thousands bow.

Three stand.

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego. Everyone can see them. The king is told.

Their answer · Daniel 3:16–18

Our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king. But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up.Daniel 3:17–18

Consider

"But if not."

Their faith was not a deal: save us and we'll obey. They trusted God with the outcome, whether He rescued them or not.

What would change if your faith had a "but if not" in it?

Click 1: the music plays and the crowd bows; three gold figures stay standing. Click 2: their answer. Click 3: "But if not."

Spend time on "but if not." It is the heart of the chapter. Faith that depends on getting the outcome you want is a bargain, not faith.

Likely answers: less fear; you can stop bargaining; you can keep going when prayers aren't answered the way you hoped.

Heated seven times hotter
Daniel 3:19

Daniel 3:19–23

The king is furious. He orders the furnace heated seven times hotter.

It is so hot that the soldiers who throw them in are killed by the heat. The three are bound and dropped into the flames.

Daniel 3:24–25

Then the king jumps to his feet.

Did not we cast three men bound into the midst of the fire?… Lo, I see four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire, and they have no hurt; and the form of the fourth is like the Son of God.Daniel 3:24–25

Daniel 3:26–27

Upon whose bodies the fire had no power, nor was an hair of their head singed, neither were their coats changed, nor the smell of fire had passed on them.Daniel 3:27

He did not keep them out of the fire.
He was in it with them.

Consider

When has the Lord been with you in a hard thing, rather than taking it away?

The king's confession · Daniel 3:28–29

Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego, who hath sent his angel, and delivered his servants that trusted in him… because there is no other God that can deliver after this sort.Daniel 3:28–29

Click 1: the flames roar up and the three men drop in. Click 2: a fourth, glowing figure appears beside them. Click 3: not even the smell of fire. Click 4: the question. Click 5: the king's confession, which is the title of the lesson.

Let the fourth figure sit on screen for a moment before you say anything.

On the question, be ready to share your own example: a loss, an illness, a move, something that didn't go away but that you weren't alone in.

Quick slide, about 90 seconds. Click 1: the tree becomes a stump and Nebuchadnezzar is humbled. Click 2: Belshazzar's feast. Click 3: the hand writes on the wall, one word at a time. Click 4: the city falls and the verse.

Nebuchadnezzar was humbled and came back. Belshazzar was warned and ignored it. Same God, same lesson, two endings.

Story Four

The Lions' Den

Daniel 6

A new empire, a new king, and Daniel is now an old man.

The den of lions

Daniel 6:1–9

Daniel is now about eighty, and the top official under King Darius of Persia.

The other officials are jealous. They dig for dirt and find none: "he was faithful" (6:4). So they build a trap. A new law: for thirty days, no one may pray to anyone but the king.

Daniel 6:10

He went into his house; and his windows being open… he kneeled upon his knees three times a day, and prayed, and gave thanks before his God, as he did aforetime.Daniel 6:10

Windows open. Nothing hidden. Nothing changed.

Daniel 6:16–18

Into the den.

Even the king, who was tricked into it, hopes out loud: "Thy God whom thou servest continually, he will deliver thee." Then he goes home and cannot eat or sleep.

Morning · Daniel 6:19–23

My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me.Daniel 6:22

"No manner of hurt was found upon him, because he believed in his God" (6:23).

Consider

Daniel's habit at fifteen was still his habit at eighty.

He did not pray harder because of the law. He prayed the way he always had, and that is what saved him.

What habit are you building now that you want to still have at eighty?

Click 1: Daniel 6:10. Click 2: the stone rolls over the den, Daniel is inside, and lions' eyes glow in the dark. Click 3: morning. The sky turns to dawn, the stone rolls away, light pours in, and the lions lie down. Click 4: the question.

"As he did aforetime" is the key phrase. The law didn't make Daniel brave; his habits did.

Likely answers: daily prayer, scripture study, church every week, honesty, keeping the Sabbath.

Daniel 7:13–14

I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven… And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom… his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away.Daniel 7:13–14

"Son of man" became Jesus's favorite name for Himself. "The Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45).

The "Ancient of days" is Adam. Before the Second Coming, Adam will gather his family at Adam-ondi-Ahman, and the Savior will come to them (D&C 116; 27:11).

Every kingdom in Daniel falls. Christ's kingdom never will.

Daniel 7 is a vision of the end: beasts rise and fall, then the Son of man comes in the clouds and receives a kingdom that never ends.

Click 1: Jesus used this title for Himself more than any other. Click 2: the Ancient of days is Adam, and Adam-ondi-Ahman is in Missouri. Click 3: the gold line.

Keep this to 90 seconds. The point is simple: the statue falls, the beasts fall, Babylon falls, Persia falls. Christ's kingdom is the only one that lasts.

Out of time? Press W from any slide to jump here, then read this aloud (about 2 minutes):

"Here's the whole lesson in two minutes. Around 605 B.C., Babylon carried off a group of bright teenagers from Jerusalem, including Daniel and his three friends. They were given new names after Babylon's gods and the best food in the empire. Daniel had already decided, before the food was in front of him, that he would not take it. He asked for ten days on plain food and water, and at the end he and his friends were healthier and ten times wiser than anyone in the king's court. Decide before the moment comes.

"The king dreamed of a giant statue made of gold, silver, brass, and iron, every part a kingdom. A stone cut out of a mountain without hands smashed it and grew to fill the whole earth. That stone is God's kingdom, and the Doctrine and Covenants says it's rolling forth in our day. You're part of it.

"Then the king built a golden statue and ordered everyone to bow. Three friends stood. They told him God was able to deliver them, 'but if not,' they still wouldn't bow. They were thrown into a furnace heated seven times hotter, and the king saw four men walking in the fire. They came out without even the smell of smoke. God didn't keep them out of the fire; He was in it with them. The king said, 'There is no other God that can deliver after this sort.'

"Years later, Daniel, now about eighty, kept praying three times a day when a law said he couldn't. He was thrown to the lions, and God shut their mouths, because he believed in his God. The habit he built at fifteen was still there at eighty.

"And Daniel saw the end: every kingdom falls, and the Son of Man, Jesus Christ, comes in the clouds to receive a kingdom that never ends.

"So: decide now. Trust Him either way. Pray like you always have. He is the only God who can deliver, and He will be with you in the fire."
