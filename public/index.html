Quest 1 · Mission briefing

Captive in Babylon

Read firstThe story so far

  • Who is talking? Isaiah, a prophet in Jerusalem about 700 years before Jesus was born. He wrote in poetry, so it sounds different from a story. Expect pictures and repeated lines.
  • What's the situation? Isaiah warned that Judah would be conquered and hauled off to Babylon. It happened about a century later. These chapters were written for people who would one day sit in Babylon feeling abandoned, ashamed, and far from home.
  • What's the big idea? Everyone in captivity wanted a deliverer who could tear down Babylon's walls. God sent a very different kind of deliverer, and that is the whole point of this week.

Warm upPicture it

Israel has been dragged off to Babylon. Massive walls. No way home. For years, people have been reading Isaiah's old prophecies about a mighty deliverer.

If you were trapped behind Babylon's walls, what kind of rescuer would you be hoping for?

Plot twist. Isaiah 52–53 describes a Messiah nobody was expecting: “He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief” (Isaiah 53:3). Where some expected a lion, God sent a lamb (Isaiah 53:7).

Jesus Christ doesn't just unlock the prison. He takes our place in it. He doesn't save us from a distance. He suffers with us.

Quest 2 · Isaiah 50–52

Hope Hunt

Read firstThe story so far

  • The feeling: Israel is in captivity because of their own choices, and they feel like God has divorced them and walked away.
  • The Lord's answer: Isaiah 50 opens with Him asking, where is the divorce paper? There isn't one. “Is my hand shortened at all, that it cannot redeem?” (50:2). He hasn't left. They wandered.
  • The command: These chapters keep shouting “Awake! Stand up! Put on thy strength!” He wants captives to look forward with hope, not backward with shame.

        Quest 3 · Isaiah 53

        Lion or Lamb?

        Read firstThe story so far

        • What this chapter is: The clearest description of Jesus Christ's suffering anywhere in the Old Testament, written about 700 years before it happened. Isaiah calls Him “the servant.”
        • Why it shocked people: Captives in Babylon wanted a conqueror. This chapter describes someone rejected, wounded, and silent, who carries other people's pain instead of fighting.
        • How to read it: Slowly. Every line is about Him, and the lesson says it is also specifically about you.
        “He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief: and we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not.”Read together · Isaiah 53:3

        Quest 4 · Isaiah 54; 57:15–19

        Never Too Far

        Read firstThe story so far

        • The feeling: Captivity felt like being abandoned, and a lot of it was their own fault. Shame says, “He's done with me.”
        • Isaiah 54: The Lord speaks to Israel like a husband who refuses to give up on his wife. He admits there was “a small moment” of distance, then promises mercy and kindness that never leave.
        • Isaiah 57: He says He lives in the high and holy place, and also with anyone who is humble and sorry. He has seen their ways and still plans to heal them.
        “For a small moment have I forsaken thee; but with great mercies will I gather thee. In a little wrath I hid my face from thee for a moment; but with everlasting kindness will I have mercy on thee, saith the Lord thy Redeemer.”Read together · Isaiah 54:7–8

        GameMyth busters

        Everyone has moments of feeling far from the Lord. Some people even give up hope of being forgiven. Isaiah 54 and 57 are the chapters for exactly those moments. Bust these myths. True or false?

        Quest 5 · Isaiah 55–56

        Everyone Invited

        Read firstThe story so far

        • The old rules: Under the law of Moses, foreigners (“strangers”) and eunuchs were kept out of full temple worship. Many of them assumed God's people did not include them.
        • Isaiah 55: The Lord shouts an invitation like a street vendor: anyone thirsty, anyone broke, come and eat for free. Seek Him while He can be found.
        • Isaiah 56: He tells the outsider not to say “The Lord hath utterly separated me from his people.” His house is “an house of prayer for all people.”
        “Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath no money; come ye, buy, and eat; yea, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price.”Read together · Isaiah 55:1
