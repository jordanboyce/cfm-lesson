Welcome everyone. The whole lesson is one big turnaround: from the worst day in Jerusalem's history to God's promise that sorrow does not get the last word.

To begin

A worst day, turned around

  1. Think of a time something went really wrong, and it ended up all right after all.
  2. What, or who, turned it around?

This week: a whole nation's worst day, and God's promise to turn it into joy.

Let two or three people share. Keep it light: a lost game, a bad test, a broken phone.

Point out that when things turn around, someone usually helped. In these chapters, that someone is the Lord.

Jeremiah 38:7–10

A question

Besides ropes, Ebed-melech brought something else. What was it?

Put now these old cast clouts and rotten rags under thine armholes under the cords.Jeremiah 38:12

Why the rags?

So the ropes would not cut into Jeremiah's arms.

Ebed-melech didn't only rescue him. He thought about how the rescue would feel.

Story Three

The Worst Day

Jeremiah 39 · Lamentations 1

Everything Jeremiah warned about now happens.

About 587 B.C. · Jeremiah 39:8–9

Jerusalem falls

The Babylonian army has been camped outside the walls for more than a year…

They break through the walls and burn the houses, the king's palace, and the temple.

Most of the people are marched hundreds of miles away to Babylon.

Just as Jeremiah warned. Yet the Lord protected Jeremiah (39:11–12).

How doth the city sit solitary, that was full of people!… She weepeth sore in the night, and her tears are on her cheeks.Lamentations 1:1–2

Click to bring down the city, then lead the people away to Babylon. The last click is Lamentations 1, a book of sorrowful poems written after the fall.

The temple was burned too (Jeremiah 52:13; 2 Kings 25:9).

Lamentations 3:22–24

But then, morning

It is of the Lord's mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness. The Lord is my portion… therefore will I hope in him. Lamentations 3:22–24

Every morning is a fresh start from the Lord.

What is one mercy you noticed today?

Go around the room for the "mercy" question. Small things count: breakfast, a friend, sunshine.

Story Four

A Field Bought
in Wartime

Jeremiah 32

This one happens during the siege, before the city falls. Jeremiah is locked in the prison court.

Would you buy it?
  1. You are locked in the palace prison.
  2. Babylon's army is right outside the walls.
  3. Your cousin Hanameel offers to sell you his family field in Anathoth for seventeen pieces of silver.

Tap once for each raised hand

sealed

Jeremiah bought it. Signed, sealed, witnessed, and the deed placed in a clay jar to last "many days" (32:9–14).

Houses and fields and vineyards shall be possessed again in this land.Jeremiah 32:15

Ah Lord God!… there is nothing too hard for thee.Jeremiah 32:17

Take the vote first. Then reveal: Jeremiah bought it.

Why? It was a promise you could see: people will live, farm, and buy land here again. He believed God before it happened.

Ask: What's a way you can show you believe good days are coming, even when things look bad?

In short

The Lord turns mourning into joy

  1. His word can't be burned away.Jeremiah 36

  2. We can lift others out of the pit.Jeremiah 38

  3. His mercies are new every morning.Lamentations 3

  4. Nothing is too hard for Him.Jeremiah 32

  5. He writes His law in our hearts, through Jesus Christ.Jeremiah 31; 33

On time? Point to the list and move on (30 seconds).

Out of time? Press W from any slide to jump here, then read this aloud (about 2 minutes):

"Here's the whole lesson in two minutes. Jeremiah spent about forty years warning Jerusalem to turn back to God, and most people wouldn't listen. When the king heard the Lord's words, he cut up the scroll and burned it. But the Lord simply had Jeremiah write it again, with even more. God's word can't be burned away, and He doesn't give up on people.

"Later, Jeremiah's enemies lowered him into a muddy pit and left him there. Nobody spoke up except Ebed-melech, a servant from Ethiopia. He went to the king, gathered thirty men, and even brought old rags so the ropes wouldn't hurt Jeremiah's arms. The Lord protected Ebed-melech because he trusted the Lord. We can be like him: notice who's in a pit, and help kindly.

"Then the worst day came. Babylon broke through the walls, burned the city and the temple, and carried the people away. The book of Lamentations is full of tears about it. But right in the middle of that sadness it says, 'His compassions fail not. They are new every morning.'

"Even before the city fell, the Lord had Jeremiah buy a field, to show that people would live in the land again. Nothing is too hard for Him.

"And He made His biggest promise: someday He would write His law not on stone, but in our hearts, and a Branch would grow from David's family. That Branch is Jesus Christ.

"So here's the main point: whatever your worst day is, it doesn't get the last word. Through Jesus Christ, the Lord turns mourning into joy."
